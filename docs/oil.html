Methods, evidence and your data

How the weekly oil-flow figures are calculated
Your original HTML defines one oil-flow rate for each month, from February to October 2026. This page preserves those monthly assumptions and allocates each daily rate into calendar weeks (Monday–Sunday). If a week spans two months, the average is weighted by days in each month. Thus, it is a weekly view of monthly assumptions—not independently collected weekly shipping data. The first window is February 1 only. The 20.7 mb/d comparison comes from the EIA's 2024 Hormuz estimate (2025 estimates from the IEA are closer to 20 mb/d). The original file's convoy numbers, tanker identities, war-risk premiums, bypass percentage and fictional vessel tracking were not retained as verified facts.
What the AIS observations do—and do not—tell us
Tankers/week represents satellite AIS-visible tanker crossings, not barrels. The external dataset uses IMF PortWatch vessel observations summarized into Monday–Sunday weeks, published through September 20, 2026. The series is absent after that date; blank values are intentionally not zero. Dark transits, AIS interference and different methodologies can cause vessel counts to diverge sharply from actual oil flows. Do not equate them. The source is credited below.
Replace modeled oil rates with weekly values (CSV)
Paste records in week_start,mbd format. Matching dates replace that week's rate; unmatched weeks remain from the scenario. Example: 2026-03-02,2.3. Enter the date of the Monday starting the week, except the first displayed window is 2026-01-26 (February 1 only). Imported figures are marked User CSV; they are not independently verified here.
