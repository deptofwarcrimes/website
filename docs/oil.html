07 / DATA SOURCES & EDITING

Methods, evidence and your data

Published oil estimates are kept separate from weekly AIS vessel passages.

How oil-flow figures are shown
This page does not manufacture monthly or weekly oil-flow values. It shows published period averages only: EIA estimates based on Vortexa tracking for 1Q and 2Q 2026, plus the IEA’s published September 2026 estimate. Those averages are displayed across the corresponding source period. The optional “derived volume” is simply the published daily average multiplied by days in the displayed window; it is not a measurement of that particular week. Similarly, any difference from the 2025 baseline is illustrative, not an observed weekly shortfall. Weeks without a matching published estimate remain blank.
What the AIS observations do—and do not—tell us
PortWatch tankers/week represents satellite AIS-visible tanker crossings, not barrels. The PortWatch series uses IMF PortWatch AIS vessel observations summarized into Monday–Sunday weeks, published through September 20, 2026. The separate Lloyd’s List series uses independently tracked non-Iranian-linked cargo vessels over 10,000 dwt and covers selected complete weeks through October 25, 2026. These are not interchangeable series: PortWatch misses dark ships, while Lloyd’s may verify movements using other intelligence and excludes Iranian-linked ships. The series is absent after that date; blank values are intentionally not zero. Dark transits, AIS interference and different methodologies can cause vessel counts to diverge sharply from actual oil flows. Do not use an AIS count to calculate cargo volume.
Add a verified weekly oil dataset (CSV)
Paste records in week_start,mbd format. Matching dates replace that week’s displayed estimate; unmatched weeks retain published estimates or remain blank. Example: 2026-03-02,2.3.
