A Delaware LLC can be a normal, lawful business. But when the public tries to learn who owns one, who funds it, and what it pays for, the answers may stop at the company name. Explore a fictional $250,000 scenario to see why.

Important: Private is not the same as secret or illegal. Banks, tax authorities, regulators and courts may have access to information that ordinary people cannot see. And certain political or other regulated spending must be disclosed.

Where the visibility gaps come from

01 / STATE FORMATION

Owner names aren't standard filing fields

Delaware's LLC formation certificate generally requires the company name and its registered agent, not a public list of members. LLCs pay an annual tax, currently $400 for tax year 2026, but do not file a Delaware annual report.

02 / FEDERAL OWNERSHIP RULE

Domestic BOI reporting was removed

FinCEN's August 2026 final rule exempts U.S.-formed entities from Corporate Transparency Act beneficial-ownership reporting. That federal exemption does not erase other bank, tax, or investigative record requirements.

03 / PRIVATE SPENDING

An invoice isn't a public filing

Ordinary private invoices and bank transfers are not posted to a universal public spending database. However, election spending, regulated advertising, tax filings, lawsuits, and other situations can require disclosures.

What if the LLC pays for political messages?

Special rules may apply. Federal campaign contributions, independent expenditures, and some election-related communications can require reports and disclaimers. LLC contributions have attribution rules, too. An LLC cannot simply ignore those requirements by operating through another name. The precise disclosure depends on who spends the money, where, and what the message says.
