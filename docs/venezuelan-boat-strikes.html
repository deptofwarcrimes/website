Timeline & Strategic Analysis of U.S. Military Strikes on Venezuelan and Regional Vessels

Between September 2025 and October 2026, the United States armed forces initiated an unprecedented campaign of lethal air-to-surface missile strikes against civilian speedboats and maritime craft in the Caribbean Sea and Eastern Pacific Ocean. Formally designated as Operation Southern Spear (OSS) and later institutionalized under Joint Task Force Western Hemisphere (JTF-WH), the campaign fundamentally replaced standard law enforcement interdiction on the high seas with wartime targeting doctrine.

3. Forensic Victim Analysis & Target Disconnect

While the Department of War characterized those killed as hardened "narco-terrorists," field investigations by journalistic consortia (El CLIP, LA Times, Associated Press, Airwars) identified 23 named victims. These investigations revealed that those killed were primarily coastal mariners, contracted boatmen (lancheros), or migrants rather than cartel bosses.

The Güiria Connection

Every identified Venezuelan victim originated from Güiria (Paria Peninsula). In eastern Venezuela, Güiria is a mixed-transit hub where lancheros simultaneously ferry migrants, fuel, consumer goods, and illicit packages across the narrow strait to Trinidad.

Pacific Fishing Communities

In the Pacific, named dead included eight fishermen from the small Mexican village of La Cruz de Huanacaxtle (Nayarit) killed on Feb 16, 2026, and mariners from Manta (Ecuador) and La Guajira (Colombia).

Intelligence Elimination

By destroying boats with missiles rather than conducting interdictions and arrests, U.S. forces eliminated the opportunity to interrogate crew members to map upstream cartel financing and leadership networks.

4. Legal Architecture, War Powers & Command Accountability

The campaign triggered severe constitutional and international legal clashes regarding the classification of drug smuggling as an armed conflict, the evasion of the War Powers Resolution, and individual criminal liability under the Law of Armed Conflict (LOAC).
